ABOUT

I did not come to music to make the silence disappear.

The quieter I listened, the more I understood that a note does not end at its edge. What comes before it, what follows it, and how honestly it is played can matter just as much.

THE STORY

Persian memory, present tense.

I am an Iranian composer and Persian setar artist based in Vienna. The setar is the thread that keeps returning in my work: intimate, fragile, capable of carrying more than it seems to at first.

I grew up with a musical culture in which a single phrase can carry memory, distance and longing without needing to explain any of them. Living in Austria changed the landscape around that memory. I did not want to choose between preserving a tradition and moving forward. I wanted to listen closely enough that both could remain alive.

That is why my music often sits somewhere between Persian instrumental language, ambient space and contemporary cinematic feeling. I am not trying to make the setar sound less Persian. I am trying to let it meet the present without costume.

HOW I WORK

Less display. More attention.

01 · SOUND

A phrase should earn its place.

I would rather leave a space open than fill it because an arrangement feels unfinished. Restraint is not absence; it is a decision about what deserves to remain.

02 · TRADITION

Roots are not a museum.

I respect what came before me by listening to it, not by freezing it. A living tradition can enter new rooms and still remember where it came from.

03 · LISTENER

I leave something unfinished on purpose.

Not every feeling needs to be named. I want the listener to have enough room to bring their own memory into the piece.

ARTIST NOTE

“A quiet sound is not a small sound. Sometimes it simply asks you to come closer.”

That is the kind of closeness I am looking for: not spectacle, not distance, but the moment when music stops performing at the listener and begins to share a room with them.
