TRACE OF A GAZELLE

Leave less behind. Listen more closely.

A solo Persian setar improvisation that does not hurry toward a climax. The piece follows breath, resonance and the small spaces where a note has ended but listening has not.

Trace of a Gazelle

Some traces are visible because they are deep. Others remain because they are light enough not to disturb what they pass through. This piece belongs to the second kind.

The setar is left close to its natural voice. Phrases arrive without being forced into a dramatic arc; pauses are allowed to carry their own weight. The music asks for attention rather than spectacle.

Silence is not what remains after the music. Here, it is part of the music.
